# Chapter 19 — Signs of Real and Fake Interest

Seattle, back in John Gottman's "Love Lab" (Chapter 3). This time, the team isn't looking for contempt. It's looking for something much smaller.

One partner says, looking out of the window: *"Look, a pretty bird."* It isn't a conversation about birds. It's a request — an **invitation** for the other's attention. Gottman called these small moments **bids**: attempts to connect.

And the other's response can go three ways: turning **towards** ("where?"), turning **against** ("I'm reading") or simply **ignoring** it.

Six years later, the couples who were still married had turned towards about **86%** of the time. Those who had divorced, about **33%** (according to data from Gottman's newlywed study).

It wasn't the grand declarations that separated the couples. It was the birds.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

Wanting to know whether someone is interested is one of the oldest questions in the world — and the answer is almost never in what they say. Gottman's study shows why: **real interest lives in small, repeated responses**, not intense moments.

And this connects to everything you saw in Part II. Love bombing (Chapter 10) is made of huge moments. Intermittent reinforcement (Chapter 14) is made of peaks. Real interest is made of 86% "where?".

In other words: **if you only recognise fire, you'll always choose the person who burns you.**

### SIGNS OF GENUINE INTEREST (TOGETHER, NOT ALONE)

- **Attention to detail** — remembers what you said days ago.
- **Body orientation** — feet and torso turned towards you (Chapter 4).
- **Initiative** — seeks you out without needing an excuse.
- **Time** — stays longer than they needed to.
- **Questions** — wants to know about you, not just talk about themselves.
- **Consistency** — the interest holds by day and by night, in public and in private.
- **Natural mirroring** — copies your posture with a delay, without realising (the chameleon effect from Chapter 4).

### SIGNS OF FAKE OR STRATEGIC INTEREST

- **Interest that only shows up when they need something.**
- **Full attention in private, distance in public.**
- **Lots of praise, little curiosity** — says you're amazing, but knows nothing about you.
- **Intensity without consistency** — days of full attention followed by silence.
- **Available only at certain hours** — usually late at night (Chapter 6).
- **Promises without dates** — "we have to go…", "one day we'll…".

### THE RIGHT QUESTION

Don't ask "does this person like me?". Ask: **"does this person treat me consistently — when no one's watching, even when they don't need anything?"**

> Real interest is boring in its consistency. Fake interest is exciting in its unpredictability.

Consistency is the best sign that someone is with you. So when someone's consistency suddenly changes, it's the first sign they may be leaving. That's Chapter 20.

EPISODE 19 · TUESDAY NIGHT
It's an ordinary Tuesday. Inês sends Daniel a photo of a cat on the wall opposite the office. He replies twenty minutes later, as always: "Looks like Tobias!" — the cat he had as a kid, which he told her about on their second date, in September.

At the same time, Sofia doesn't answer a work question from Inês. The next day, she turns up with two coffees and a box of chocolates: "Sorry about yesterday, darling. You know you're my person."

Inês notices she never knows which Sofia she'll find in the morning.

*Who remembered September?*
