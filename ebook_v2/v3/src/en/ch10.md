# Chapter 10 — Love Bombing: the Love That Traps You

He introduced himself as Simon Leviev, son of a diamond tycoon. His real name was Shimon Hayut. He met women on Tinder and, on the first date, flew them by private jet to another country.

Then came the messages at all hours. The good-morning voice notes. The fast declarations. The plans for the future — a house, travel, children.

Weeks later came the crisis: "my enemies are after me", blocked cards, an urgent request for money "just for a few days". According to the documentary *The Tinder Swindler* (Netflix, 2022), he took around $10 million from his victims.

The private jet wasn't the trick. The **speed** was.

### WHAT IT IS

Love bombing is a flood of attention, affection and promises at the start of a relationship — romantic, a friendship or even professional. The goal isn't to love you. It's to create **emotional dependence fast**, before you've had time to see who the person is.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

For a long time, "love bombing" was just an internet phrase. In 2017, Claire Strutzenberg and colleagues ran one of the first studies on it, with 484 young adults. Love bombing was linked to **more narcissism** and **lower self-esteem** in the person doing it — and to more texting and social media in the relationship (Strutzenberg et al., 2017).

In other words: behind the excess of love there's often someone who needs to **control** — and who feels small inside. It's the narcissist from Chapter 9, with flowers in hand.

And that's exactly why it works: it uses what we want most — to be seen and loved — as bait. People who don't recognise it mistake the trap for the best moment of their life, and spend the following months trying to get back there.

### THE STAGES

:::fig 6:::

1. **Idealisation** — you're perfect. No one understands you like he does. Everything is intense, fast, magical.
2. **Dependence** — you get used to that dose of attention. Your day starts revolving around it.
3. **Devaluation** — the attention starts slipping. Criticism, coldness, comparisons appear. And you work hard to get back to stage 1.
4. **Discard or cycle** — either they drop you, or they alternate hot and cold to keep you hooked. You'll see why this hooks so hard in Chapter 14.

### THE SIGNS

- **Speed** — intimacy that should take months happens in days.
- **Disproportionate intensity** — expensive gifts, huge declarations, "soulmates" too early.
- **Fast-forwarded future** — moving in, trips, children, business partnerships, before there's any foundation.
- **Total attention** — constant messages, always available, wants to know everything.
- **Discomfort with your "no"** — when you slow down, they're hurt, they push, or they blame you.
- **Little real history** — you know a lot about what they feel for you and little about their life.

The last one is the most ignored — and the most important. In the *Tinder Swindler* cases, the victims knew everything about his feelings and almost nothing they could **verify**. It's the gap from Chapter 1: lots of declaration, little repeated behaviour.

### REAL LOVE VS. LOVE BOMBING

| Real love | Love bombing |
|---|---|
| Grows over time | Explodes at once |
| Respects your pace | Pushes your pace |
| Wants to know you | Wants to have you |
| Accepts a "no" | Punishes a "no" |
| Is consistent | Is intense, then unstable |

> People who like you have time. People who want to trap you are in a hurry.

### HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF

- **You slow down.** If the other person won't accept your pace, you already have your answer.
- **Notice the discomfort with "no".** It's the fastest test (remember boundaries, in Chapter 4).
- **Ask about the past.** How did their previous relationships end? How do they talk about their exes? (Chapter 9.)
- **Give it time.** Love bombing rarely survives a few months without showing stage 3.

Love bombing opens the door. The next step is locking you inside — away from the people who could warn you. That's Chapter 11.

:::usa
[protege]
The 90-day rule: no big decisions — money, moving in, cutting someone off — in the first three months. People who like you will wait.
[ajuda]
If a friend is "on cloud nine" with someone they met weeks ago, don't attack that person. Ask: "And how does he react when you tell him no?" A question plants a doubt. An attack forces your friend to choose.
:::

:::treino
Write down three paces you think are healthy at the start of a relationship — for example, "meeting his friends only after a month". Next time someone tries to speed things up, you'll have a pattern to compare against.
