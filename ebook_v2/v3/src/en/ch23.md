# Chapter 23 — How to Leave and Not Go Back

Dallas, 3 July 1976. Tina Turner walks out of a downtown hotel, runs across a motorway and goes into another hotel on the other side. She has 36 cents in her pocket and a fuel card. She's injured. The manager recognises her and hides her for three days.

She was already a global star. But she had spent years in a violent, controlling marriage — and, like so many people, she hadn't left the first time, or the second.

That night she left. And she didn't go back. Years later, she would have the biggest phase of her career — under her own name.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

Leaving a manipulative relationship — romantic, professional or a friendship — is harder than it looks from outside. Not because the person is weak, but because **the cycle was built to hold you**: it's Dutton and Painter's traumatic bonding (Chapter 14), made of an imbalance of power and good days among the bad.

And the moment of leaving is when the cycle fights hardest. That's why many people who leave without a plan go back — and every attempt, even a failed one, counts. Notice what Tina Turner did that night: she didn't argue, didn't negotiate, didn't explain. **She left, and went somewhere safe.**

In other words: leaving isn't a conversation. It's a plan.

### BEFORE YOU LEAVE

- **Decide quietly.** Don't announce your intention before you're ready. Many manipulative people tighten control when they feel they're losing you.
- **Sort out the essentials.** Documents, money, access to your accounts, changed passwords, somewhere to go.
- **Tell your inner circle** (Chapter 22). Two people who know what's going on. It's Asch's ally (Chapter 7), for the hardest decision.
- **Write down your reasons.** A short, honest list. You'll need it on the days you miss them.

### WHEN YOU LEAVE

- **Short and clear.** "This relationship is over." You don't need to convince anyone or win the final argument.
- **Don't negotiate.** Promises to change at this moment are part of the cycle.
- **Somewhere safe**, if there's any risk at all. Your safety comes first.

### AFTER YOU LEAVE

- **No contact, wherever possible.** Block, mute, unfollow. Every message you read reopens the wound — it's intermittent reward again (Chapter 14).
- **Minimal contact, where it's unavoidable** (children, work): practical matters only, in writing, brief, without emotion.
- **Expect the hoovering** (Chapter 15). When it comes, you're not surprised — you're prepared.
- **Reread your list** when the nostalgia hits.
- **Rebuild your life** with people, routines and goals that are your own.

> Leaving hurts for a while. Staying hurts for years.

### AN IMPORTANT NOTE

If you're in a situation involving violence, threats or danger, get specialist help. Most countries have a free, confidential domestic abuse or victim support helpline — search for the one where you live and save the number. In immediate danger, call your local emergency number (**911** in the US, **112** in Europe, **999** in the UK). You're not alone, and you don't have to sort this out without help.

Leaving is the decision of the person inside. But people inside rarely leave alone — they leave because someone outside never stopped being there. That someone could be you. That's Chapter 24.
