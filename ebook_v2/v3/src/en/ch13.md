# Chapter 13 — Triangulation and Provoked Jealousy

London, November 1995. In a BBC interview watched by millions, Princess Diana talks about her marriage to Prince Charles and says a line that went down in history: *"There were three of us in this marriage, so it was a bit crowded."*

Whose fault it was doesn't matter here. What matters is the shape: a relationship of two that comes to be lived as three — with someone always present, even when they're not in the room.

When that third person is brought in **on purpose**, to create insecurity and control, it's called **triangulation**.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

Psychiatrist Murray Bowen spent decades studying whole families and reached a conclusion that applies to almost every relationship: when tension between two people rises, one of them tends to **pull in a third** to relieve the pressure — a child, a friend, an ex, a colleague (Bowen, 1978). Triangles are the smallest stable unit of a relationship system.

Often it happens without intent. But manipulators use it **on purpose** — and the effect is always the same: instead of looking at what's going on between the two of you, you start looking at the third person.

In other words: while you're comparing yourself, you don't notice what's being done to you.

### HOW IT SHOWS UP

- **Constantly talking about an ex**, in an ambiguous way.
- **Praising someone else in front of you** — "She's so calm, nothing ever bothers her."
- **Answering other people's messages** while ignoring yours (remember Chapter 6).
- **Mentioning that "someone" is interested.**
- **At work** — "your colleague did this in half the time".
- **In the family** — comparing siblings.

### THE GOAL

1. **Getting you to compete** — you start working to be "better" than the third person.
2. **Shifting the focus** — instead of looking at their behaviour, you look at the threat.
3. **Gaining power** — whoever provokes jealousy controls the relationship.
4. **Making you look unstable** — if you react, you're "jealous" and "possessive". And here triangulation joins up with the gaslighting of Chapter 12.

### THE SIGNS

- **You feel jealousy** in a relationship where you didn't before — a deviation from *your* baseline (Chapter 2).
- **The third person comes up in conversation** strangely often.
- **When you complain**, the answer is "you're imagining things".
- **You feel you're always being compared.**

> People who value you don't enter you in a contest.

### HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF

- **Name the pattern calmly, once** — "I notice you talk about her a lot when we've fallen out. Why?"
- **Don't compete.** The moment you play the game, you've lost.
- **Notice whether the behaviour stops** when you point it out. If it carries on or gets worse, it wasn't an accident.

Triangulation messes with your place. But there's an even stronger tactic — one that messes with your brain chemistry and explains why so many people can't leave. That's Chapter 14.
