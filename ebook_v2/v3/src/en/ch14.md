# Chapter 14 — Silence and Intermittent Reinforcement: Why You Can't Leave

Harvard University, the 1950s. Psychologist B. F. Skinner trains pigeons to peck a button for food.

When the food comes **every time**, the pigeons peck as much as they need to — and if the food stops, they give up quickly.

When the food comes **unpredictably** — sometimes on the first peck, sometimes on the twentieth — something else happens. The pigeons peck non-stop. And they keep pecking long after the food has stopped coming (Ferster & Skinner, 1957).

It's the same mechanism as slot machines. And certain relationships.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

The question that causes most shame for people in a relationship that's hurting them is: *"Why don't you just leave?"*

In 1981, psychologists Donald Dutton and Susan Painter gave an answer that took the blame off victims. They called it **traumatic bonding**: a bond that forms — and grows stronger — when there's an **imbalance of power** and when good and bad treatment **alternate** (Dutton & Painter, 1981). It isn't weakness. It's conditioning — the same thing that kept the pigeons on the button.

In other words: the good moments don't make up for the bad ones. **They're what hooks you.**

### THE SILENT TREATMENT

Stopping talking to you as punishment. Ignoring messages, answering in single words, acting as if you don't exist — for hours or days — until you give in.

Don't confuse it with needing space:

| Healthy space | Silent treatment |
|---|---|
| "I need to think, let's talk tomorrow." | Silence with no explanation. |
| Has a clear end. | Lasts until you give in. |
| Is for calming down. | Is for punishing. |

Notice what silence does: it suddenly takes the reward away. Then, when it comes back, the reward tastes twice as good — it's the pigeon's button again.

### WHY IT HOOKS YOU

- **The good moments feel even better** after the bad ones.
- **You start working to "earn"** the good version.
- **You blame yourself for the bad moments** — "if I were different…"
- **You remember the peaks** more than the average.

> You're not hooked on the person. You're hooked on the hope of getting back to the beginning.

### THE SIGNS

- **The relationship feels like a rollercoaster.**
- **You spend more time analysing** the person's behaviour than living the relationship.
- **Your mood depends on theirs.**
- **You're "addicted" to the make-ups.**

### HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF

- **Judge the average, not the peaks.** What's the relationship like on an ordinary day?
- **Write down the cycles** — when it was good, when it was bad, what changed. It's the record from Chapter 12 with a different purpose: seeing the pattern.
- **Don't reward silence.** Don't chase someone who punishes you with absence. Keep your life going.

And when you finally step away, the cycle has one last card to play. The food drops again, suddenly, after weeks of nothing. That's Chapter 15.
