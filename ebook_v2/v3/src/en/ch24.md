# Chapter 24 — How to Help Someone Who's Trapped

New York University, 1968. A student sits alone in a room, talking over an intercom with other participants, each in their own room. Suddenly, one of them starts having what sounds like an epileptic seizure. He asks for help, his voice failing.

What researchers John Darley and Bibb Latané wanted to know was simple: **who leaves the room to help?**

When the student thought he was the only one listening, **85%** went to help. When he thought four other people were listening, only **31%** did (Darley & Latané, 1968).

It wasn't a lack of compassion. It was the feeling that **someone else would deal with it**. They called it **diffusion of responsibility**.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

When a friend is trapped in a relationship that's destroying them, there are almost always several people who notice. And almost always each one thinks someone else will do something.

And remember Asch (Chapter 7): when there was a single ally in the room, almost everyone broke free of group pressure. Put the two studies together and you have the central idea of this chapter: **people who are trapped rarely get out alone. They get out because someone outside decided to be the ally — instead of waiting for someone else to be.**

Everything you've read so far is for protecting yourself. This chapter is for protecting the people you love.

### WHAT DOESN'T WORK

- **Attacking the person** — it forces your friend to defend them. It's the isolation of Chapter 11 working in the manipulator's favour.
- **Ultimatums** — "it's them or me" pushes them further in.
- **Labels** — "narcissist", "psychopath" sound like exaggeration and shut the conversation down (Chapter 9).
- **Rushing** — the decision to leave has to be theirs, in their own time.

### WHAT WORKS

1. **Stay.** Your presence is exactly what isolation wants to destroy. Keep in touch, even if it's one message a week.
2. **Ask, don't tell.** "How do you feel after you've been together?" "What happens when you tell them no?" Questions let them reach the conclusions themselves — and their own conclusions are the only ones that stick.
3. **Talk about behaviour, not character.** "I've noticed you don't come to dinners any more", instead of "he's pulling you away from us".
4. **Validate what they feel.** "It makes sense that you're confused." People living with gaslighting need someone to confirm their reality (Chapter 12).
5. **Remind them who they are.** Talk about the things they loved, the people they were close to. The manipulator rewrote their identity. You keep the original.
6. **Help with practical things when they ask.** A place to stay, help with a move, company for a difficult conversation.

> You don't need to rescue them. You need to be the place they can come back to.

### WHEN THERE'S DANGER

If there's violence, threats or real fear, safety comes first. Domestic abuse and victim support helplines also support people who want to help someone else — look up the one in your country. In immediate danger, call your local emergency number (**911** in the US, **112** in Europe, **999** in the UK).

### AND YOU

Helping someone who's trapped is tiring. Here too, you have the right to boundaries (Chapter 22): you can be available without being available all the time. Look after your energy so you can keep going.

You now know how to see, respond, set boundaries, leave and help. What's left is the part that turns all of this into a reflex: training. That's Chapter 25.

EPISODE 24 · RUI
One Sunday afternoon, Inês runs into Rui on the stairs, with shopping bags. This time, he's the one who stops.

"Sorry to butt in. Two years ago, my neighbour on the third floor, Beatriz, worked at your agency. Sofia used to come here a lot. At first they were inseparable." He pauses. "After a year, Beatriz didn't talk to anyone. Not even her family. One day she moved to Coimbra and I never heard from her again. I never said anything to her. I thought it was none of my business."

He puts the bags down. "I didn't want to do the same with you. Can I just ask you one thing? How do you feel after you've been with her?"
