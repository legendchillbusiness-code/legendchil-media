# Chapter 25 — The 7-Day Calibration Protocol

Berlin, the early 1990s. At the city's music academy, psychologist Anders Ericsson and colleagues ask young violinists to reconstruct, year by year, how much time they'd spent practising since childhood. They compare the ones their teachers considered the best with the ones who were merely good.

The difference wasn't in initial talent, or in hours spent with a violin in hand. It was in the type of training: the best had built up far more hours of what Ericsson called **deliberate practice** — training focused on one thing at a time, with a clear goal and feedback (Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer, 1993).

Reading people is the same. It doesn't improve with reading. It improves with deliberate practice.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

Remember the first number in this book: on average, we're right 54% of the time when we try to detect lies (Chapter 1). And the second: from 30 seconds of silent video, strangers already rate people almost as well as those who've known them for months (Chapter 4). In other words: you already read people — just without a method or training.

The protocol in this chapter does exactly what Ericsson described: **one channel at a time, with a goal, and with confirmation at the end**. Without it, this book becomes information you forget in two weeks. With seven days, it becomes reflex.

### DAY 1 — BASELINE

Pick three people you talk to today. For each one, note: speech pace, gestures, eye contact. Just the normal (Chapter 2).

### DAY 2 — NO SOUND

Watch 10 minutes of an interview with the sound off. Note expressions and posture changes. Then watch with sound and compare (Chapter 3).

### DAY 3 — THE FEET

All day long, look only at the feet of people in conversation. Who wants to stay? Who wants to leave? (Chapter 4)

### DAY 4 — THE WORDS

In a conversation, count: questions answered with questions, "honestly", long explanations for simple questions (Chapter 5).

### DAY 5 — THE ROOM

At a meeting, dinner or group, identify the Alpha, the Shadow, the Performer and the Observer in the first two minutes (Chapter 7).

### DAY 6 — THE TRIGGERS

Notice every time someone tried to influence you today — ads, colleagues, family. Which trigger did they use? (Chapter 16)

### DAY 7 — YOU

Today, watch yourself. When did you feel uncomfortable? What gestures did you make? What words did you use to dodge a topic? Remember your open doors (Chapter 8). People who know themselves read others better.

> After seven days, you won't read everyone. But you'll stop not seeing.

### AFTER THE 7 DAYS

Repeat the protocol once a month. It's Ericsson's rule: what counts isn't the number of hours, it's the quality of the practice. Each time you'll notice faster and more accurately.
