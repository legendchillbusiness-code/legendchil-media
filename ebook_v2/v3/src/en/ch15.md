# Chapter 15 — Hoovering: the Comeback

You left. Or they did. Weeks have passed. And suddenly:

*"I've been thinking about you a lot." "I've changed. I've been in therapy." "I found a photo of us." "I just wanted to know if you're OK."*

The name comes from Hoover vacuum cleaners: **hoovering** is the attempt to "suck" you back into the relationship. And it's far more common than it looks.

When researcher René Dailey and colleagues studied on-again/off-again relationships, they found that **almost two in three** participants had been in a relationship that ended and started again. And those relationships, compared with stable ones, had **more uncertainty and more negative features** (Dailey et al., 2009).

Not every comeback is manipulation. But the comeback is the moment when a bad pattern has its best chance of starting over.

### WHY THIS MATTERS

Remember Skinner's pigeons, in Chapter 14: what kept them pecking wasn't the food — it was the **possibility** of it coming back. Hoovering is exactly that: after weeks of nothing, the reward drops again. And the brain, which had started to give up, lights back up.

In other words: the most dangerous moment often isn't the relationship. **It's the comeback.** It arrives when you're recovering, with the right words — and one "yes" throws away months of work.

### WHY IT HAPPENS

It's not necessarily love. Often it's:

- **The new source of attention fell through.**
- **They need to confirm they still have power** over you.
- **They're bored** — remember the functional psychopath, in Chapter 9.
- **They need something** — money, a place to stay, contacts, ego.

### THE FORMS

- **Nostalgia** — photos, songs, "remember when…".
- **Dramatic regret** — promises of change without actions.
- **Crisis** — illness, problems, "I've got no one else". It's the Victim Mask (Chapter 1).
- **Practical excuses** — "I left something of mine at your place".
- **Through others** — they reappear via your friends. It's triangulation (Chapter 13) in reverse.
- **Special dates** — birthdays and holidays are favourite moments.

### REAL CHANGE OR HOOVERING?

- **Real change comes with actions over time**, not intense words.
- **Real change respects your "no" the first time.**
- **Real change takes concrete responsibility** — not "mistakes were made" (the distancing from Chapter 5), but "I did this".
- **Hoovering vanishes once it gets what it wants** — and the cycle starts over.

> If the only thing that changed is the message, nothing changed.

### HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF

- **Decide in advance.** Write down now, with a cool head, what you'll do if the person comes back. When it happens, follow what you wrote.
- **Don't reply in the moment.** Wait 24 hours before any reply.
- **Notice the timing.** Do they come back just when you're recovering? That's strategy.
- **Remember the average**, not the best days.
